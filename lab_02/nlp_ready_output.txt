introduction to natural language processing natural language processing nlp is a field of artificial intelligence that focuses on the interaction between computers and human language
it is used in search engines chatbots and translation systems
the field began in the s and has grown rapidly since
today nlp powers applications that millions of people use every day
language models learn patterns from large text corpora
text preprocessing text preprocessing is the first step in any nlp pipeline
common steps include tokenization lowercasing stopword removal and stemming
clean data leads to better models
tokenization splits text into words or sentences
stopwords such as the is and of are frequent but carry little meaning
feature extraction machine learning models cannot read raw text so text must be converted into numbers
bag of words and tf idf are classic feature extraction methods
word embeddings capture semantic meaning in dense vectors
modern language models use contextual embeddings
these representations change depending on the surrounding words which helps with ambiguity in language
machine learning for text classification clustering and sequence labelling are common tasks
sentiment analysis predicts whether a review is positive or negative
named entity recognition finds people places and organizations in text
neural networks especially transformers have achieved state of the art results
training these models requires large datasets and powerful hardware
evaluation of nlp systems evaluation metrics include accuracy precision recall and the f score
human evaluation is still important for tasks like summarization and dialogue
a good evaluation uses a held out test set
cross validation can give a more reliable estimate of performance on small datasets
applications and future directions nlp applications include virtual assistants spam filters machine translation and document summarization
language technology is improving access to information for people worldwide
future directions include multilingual models low resource languages and more efficient training
ethical issues such as bias and fairness must also be addressed in language technology
